Airline Passenger Satisfaction Analysis & Excel Dashboard
Project Overview
This project explores airline passenger satisfaction using Microsoft Excel. It analyzes 129,880 passenger records and presents key metrics, comparisons, and service-rating patterns in an Excel dashboard.

The objective is to turn passenger data into understandable business insights that can help an airline identify areas where the customer experience may be improved.
Tools Used
- Microsoft Excel
- PivotTables and PivotCharts
- Excel formulas and summary calculations
- Data analysis and visualization

Selected Findings
- Returning customers have a higher satisfaction rate (47.8%) than first-time customers (24.0%).
- Business Class has a higher satisfaction rate (69.4%) than Economy (18.8%) and Economy Plus (24.6%).
- Business travel has a higher satisfaction rate (58.4%) than personal travel (10.1%).
- The 45–54 age group has the highest satisfaction rate (57.9%) among the displayed age groups; the 65+ group has the lowest (18.4%).
- The 3000+ flight-distance band has the highest displayed satisfaction rate (77.4%).
- In-flight Wi-Fi (2.8/5) and ease of online booking (2.9/5) are among the lowest-rated service categories.
- Neutral or dissatisfied passengers have higher average departure and arrival delays than satisfied passengers.
Business Recommendations
1. Investigate the drivers of low satisfaction in Economy and Economy Plus.
2. Review the end-to-end experience for first-time passengers.
3. Investigate the needs and expectations of personal-travel passengers.
4. Prioritize improvements to in-flight Wi-Fi and online booking usability.
5. Review operational causes of delays and strengthen passenger communication during disruptions.
6. Examine the needs of older passengers and other lower-satisfaction age segments.
Notes on Interpretation
The dashboard shows descriptive comparisons and associations. These results do not, by themselves, prove that a particular factor causes dissatisfaction. Further analysis (for example, statistical testing or a multivariable model) would be needed to assess independent effects.
